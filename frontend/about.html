About the Good Health & Well-Being Project

Our mission is to spread universal health literacy, reduce chronic lifestyle ailments, and empower people across all generations with actionable, science-grounded well-being tools.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to standard health, nutrition, and lifestyle questions.

The World Health Organization (WHO) recommends at least 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic physical activity (such as brisk walking), or at least 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity per week, plus muscle-strengthening activities on 2 or more days.
While the generic advice is 8 glasses (about 2 liters), your actual hydration need depends on your body mass, climate temperature, and daily sweat rate. You can use our Interactive Water Calculator on the Healthy Food page for an individualized recommendation.
Screens emit short-wavelength blue light which halts the secretion of melatonin, the neurohormone responsible for inducing drowsiness. This delays sleep onset, reduces REM restorative sleep, and keeps the brain's dopamine pathways hyper-stimulated.
Start with low friction. A 10-minute walk outside after lunch or dinner is a perfect start. After one week, increase to 20 minutes and add basic morning stretches. Focus on daily consistency before worrying about intensity.
